# agentme-bdr-policy-502: Control framework and standards-setters

## Context and Problem Statement

`agentme-bdr-405` assigns decision rights to product-team roles and states in rule 07 that approval authorities (Compliance, Legal, Risk) sit outside its matrix. No policy says who defines standards by risk class, who turns them into controls, and who verifies that the team followed them. Without that split, teams grade their own work and auditors cannot tell a passed control from an unchecked one.

Question: Which functions define standards and controls, which implement them and which verify them, and how is independence kept?

## Decision Outcome

**One party defines, one implements, another controls: standards-setters define standards by risk class, the product team implements them and generates evidence, and an independent Control Owner defines the controls and verifies the evidence as pass or fail**

### Details

#### 01-define-implement-control-separation

For every standard and control, three distinct parties MUST be involved: a standards-setter defines it, the product team implements it and generates the evidence, and the Control Owner verifies the evidence. One party MUST NOT fill two of these parts for the same control.

#### 02-control-owner

The Control Owner MUST define the IT general controls (ITGC) and business controls of a solution, each with an objective, a check, the evidence it needs and its frequency. Controls span risk mitigation, security, data, architecture, performance, standards compliance and incident management. The Control Owner MUST verify controls from evidence generated by the product team and record each result as pass or fail.

The Control Owner MUST always be independent from the implementers and from the standards-setters of the control being verified, even for small or low-risk applications. One Control Owner MAY serve many solutions. A Product Owner, Tech Lead or engineer of the solution MUST NOT be its Control Owner.

#### 03-risk-function

The Risk function MUST own the organisation's classification scales and risk appetite, MUST decide the risk classification process used in phase 3 of `agentme-bdr-004`, and MUST record and approve governance exceptions under `agentme-edr-503` rule 03. This policy defines no scales: each organisation chooses its own (see `agentme-bdr-004`).

#### 04-standards-setters

Standards-setters MUST publish their standards by risk class as XDRS policies, so that phase 4 can apply them to a solution:

| Function | Standards it defines |
|---|---|
| Enterprise Architect | Solution, platform, integration and security-architecture standards |
| Principal Engineer | Engineering and AI-practice standards; MUST comply with Enterprise Architect standards |
| Security | Security measures and baselines per risk class |
| Legal | Legal, contractual and regulatory requirements |
| DPO | Data-protection requirements, per `agentme-bdr-501` |

The Enterprise Architect is a cross-team role defined in `agentme-bdr-402`. Security, Legal, DPO and the Risk function are assurance functions outside the product team and outside the matrix of `agentme-bdr-405`.

#### 05-standards-become-controls

Control objectives derived from standards MUST be transformed into requirements and non-functional requirements in phase 4 of `agentme-bdr-004`. A standard that needs verification MUST have at least one control with a named Control Owner.

#### 06-unstaffed-functions

When a function in this policy is not staffed, the organisation MAY leave it unnamed only with a recorded exception that names an owner and a due date, kept in the record system. Without that exception, the phase gates of `agentme-bdr-503` MUST fail. The exception is recorded by the Risk function.
